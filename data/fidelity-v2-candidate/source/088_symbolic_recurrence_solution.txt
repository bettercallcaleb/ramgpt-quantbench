Generating functions convert a recurrence relation into an algebraic equation for a formal power series. The method is especially useful when the recurrence is linear but the forcing term has its own structure.

Consider the sequence defined by

a_0=2,
a_1=5,

and for n>=2,

a_n = 3a_{n-1} - 2a_{n-2} + n 2^n.

The homogeneous recurrence has characteristic polynomial

r^2-3r+2=(r-1)(r-2),

so one already expects homogeneous terms C_1 + C_2 2^n. The forcing n2^n resonates with the root r=2, so a direct undetermined-coefficient approach would require multiplying the usual trial polynomial by n. A generating function handles this automatically.

Define

A(z)=sum_{n>=0} a_n z^n.

Multiply the recurrence by z^n and sum over n>=2:

sum_{n>=2} a_n z^n
=
3 sum_{n>=2} a_{n-1} z^n
-2 sum_{n>=2} a_{n-2} z^n
+sum_{n>=2} n2^n z^n.

The left side is

A(z)-a_0-a_1 z
=A(z)-2-5z.

For the first shifted sum,

sum_{n>=2} a_{n-1}z^n
=z sum_{m>=1} a_m z^m
=z(A(z)-a_0)
=z(A(z)-2).

For the second,

sum_{n>=2} a_{n-2}z^n
=z^2 A(z).

The forcing term uses the standard identity

sum_{n>=0} n x^n = x/(1-x)^2.

With x=2z,

sum_{n>=0} n2^n z^n
=2z/(1-2z)^2.

The n=0 term is zero and the n=1 term is 2z, so

sum_{n>=2} n2^n z^n
=2z/(1-2z)^2 - 2z.

Substituting everything,

A-2-5z
=
3z(A-2)
-2z^2 A
+2z/(1-2z)^2
-2z.

Collect A terms:

A(1-3z+2z^2)
=
2+5z-6z-2z
+2z/(1-2z)^2.

Thus

A(1-z)(1-2z)
=
2-3z + 2z/(1-2z)^2.

Therefore

A(z)
=
(2-3z)/[(1-z)(1-2z)]
+
2z/[(1-z)(1-2z)^3].

This decomposition already reveals the solution form. The first rational term has poles at z=1 and z=1/2, while the second has a third-order pole at z=1/2. A pole of order three at 1/2 generates coefficients proportional to a quadratic polynomial times 2^n. Because of resonance, the particular solution will contain n^2 2^n and n 2^n terms.

To extract coefficients cleanly, combine the expression over the common denominator

(1-z)(1-2z)^3.

The numerator is

(2-3z)(1-2z)^2 + 2z.

Since

(1-2z)^2=1-4z+4z^2,

we get

(2-3z)(1-4z+4z^2)
=2-11z+20z^2-12z^3.

Adding 2z yields

2-9z+20z^2-12z^3.

Hence

A(z)
=
(2-9z+20z^2-12z^3) /
[(1-z)(1-2z)^3].

Write a partial-fraction form

A(z)
=
C/(1-z)
+D/(1-2z)
+E/(1-2z)^2
+F/(1-2z)^3.

Multiplying by the denominator,

2-9z+20z^2-12z^3
=
C(1-2z)^3
+D(1-z)(1-2z)^2
+E(1-z)(1-2z)
+F(1-z).

Set z=1 to isolate C:

2-9+20-12 = C(1-2)^3,

1=-C,

so C=-1.

Set z=1/2 to isolate F:

2-9/2+20/4-12/8
=F(1-1/2).

The left side is

2-4.5+5-1.5=1.

Thus F/2=1 and F=2.

The remaining D and E can be found by coefficient comparison. Expand the basis polynomials:

(1-2z)^3 = 1-6z+12z^2-8z^3,

(1-z)(1-2z)^2
=1-5z+8z^2-4z^3,

(1-z)(1-2z)
=1-3z+2z^2,

1-z.

Substitute C=-1 and F=2. The constant coefficient gives

2 = -1 + D + E + 2,

so

D+E=1.

The z^3 coefficient gives

-12 = 8 -4D,

because the E and F terms have degree at most two and one. Thus

-20=-4D,
D=5.

Therefore E=-4.

So

A(z)
=
-1/(1-z)
+5/(1-2z)
-4/(1-2z)^2
+2/(1-2z)^3.

Now use the coefficient identities

[z^n] 1/(1-z)=1,

[z^n] 1/(1-2z)=2^n,

[z^n] 1/(1-2z)^2=(n+1)2^n,

and

[z^n] 1/(1-2z)^3=binom(n+2,2)2^n.

Hence

a_n
=
-1
+5(2^n)
-4(n+1)2^n
+2 binom(n+2,2)2^n.

Since

2 binom(n+2,2)=(n+1)(n+2),

the coefficient of 2^n becomes

5 -4(n+1)+(n+1)(n+2).

Expand:

5-4n-4+n^2+3n+2
=n^2-n+3.

Therefore

a_n = (n^2-n+3)2^n - 1.

Check the initial conditions:

n=0:
a_0=(0-0+3)1-1=2.

n=1:
a_1=(1-1+3)2-1=5.

For n=2,

a_2=(4-2+3)4-1=19.

The recurrence gives

3(5)-2(2)+2(4)=15-4+8=19.

The formula is consistent.

The same generating-function machinery extends to more complicated forcing terms. Polynomial forcing gives powers of (1-z)^(-1), exponential forcing gives poles at reciprocal exponential bases, and products such as n^k alpha^n produce higher-order poles at z=1/alpha. Resonance in recurrence solving is visible as pole multiplicity after the recurrence denominator and forcing denominator interact.

This is one reason generating functions are more than a trick for extracting a closed form. They translate shifts into multiplication by z, initial conditions into low-degree corrections, and forcing terms into known rational functions. The recurrence becomes a statement about singularities of A(z), and those singularities encode the eventual algebraic form of a_n.
