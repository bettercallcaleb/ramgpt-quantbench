Dance Company Tour Logistics Meeting

Participants:
Rafael — company manager
Nora — production manager
Jules — rehearsal director
Imani — touring physiotherapist

Rafael: We have six cities in nine days, and the current schedule assumes every travel day is also a full technical day. I don't think that is physically realistic.

Nora: It isn't. The venues are not identical, and two have load-in restrictions that already compress the schedule.

Jules: The dancers also need enough warm-up time after travel. We can't unload at noon, sit on a bus for six hours, and then treat a 20-minute barre as sufficient.

Imani: Especially with the jump-heavy second act. Travel stiffness plus shortened warm-up is exactly the combination I want to avoid.

Rafael: Let's map the hard constraints first.

Nora: City one is straightforward. Truck unload at 08:00, theatre access from 08:00, show at 19:30. City two is the problem: truck cannot access the loading dock until 12:00 because of a market closure.

Jules: How long is travel from city one?

Rafael: Four hours and fifteen minutes without stops.

Imani: Then dancers should not travel on the truck schedule. They need at least one break.

Rafael: Coach leaves at 07:30, rest stop at 09:30, arrival around noon.

Jules: If theatre access starts at noon, can we rehearse onstage at 15:00?

Nora: Only if the lighting focus is already complete. That venue is receiving our pre-focus file, but they still need physical adjustment.

Rafael: What can happen without dancers?

Nora: Deck and lighting. Sound can line-check. Wardrobe can set quick-change stations.

Jules: Then dancers arrive, eat, rest, and warm up while production works.

Imani: I want a minimum 45-minute movement warm-up before any full-out stage spacing. Not 45 minutes from entering the building; 45 minutes of actual warm-up.

Rafael: Can we start stage spacing at 16:15?

Nora: Yes, if we keep it to spacing and cue checks, not a full run.

Jules: That's enough. We can mark difficult transitions.

Rafael: City three is a matinee the next day.

Imani: That's the bigger issue. What is the show finish the night before?

Nora: Curtain at 19:30, runtime 95 minutes plus bows. Out by 21:20.

Rafael: Load-out to 23:30.

Jules: Dancers don't need to stay for load-out.

Rafael: Correct. Hotel is a ten-minute walk.

Imani: Morning call for matinee?

Jules: We proposed 10:30 for a 14:00 show.

Imani: Fine if breakfast is available early and the dancers aren't required at a production meeting.

Rafael: Production meeting is staff only.

Nora: I want one dancer representative for stage-floor feedback.

Jules: We can collect that the night before.

Imani: Better.

Rafael: Transportation of costumes. Nora?

Nora: Main wardrobe travels on the truck. Critical costume pieces and one emergency kit travel separately with the company.

Rafael: Why split?

Nora: If the truck is delayed, we still have enough to perform a reduced version. The emergency kit includes shoes, principal costumes, repair supplies, and key accessories.

Jules: Can we truly perform without the full truck?

Nora: Not the full production. But we can mount a concert version if the venue and artistic director approve.

Rafael: Then we need that contingency documented rather than pretending the truck cannot be late.

Imani: Same for medical kit. My treatment supplies stay with me, not in freight.

Rafael: Agreed.

Jules: Stage floor consistency worries me. City four has a hard wood floor, not sprung.

Imani: Then we should not perform the same choreography on it without an approved sprung overlay.

Nora: Contract says venue provides a sprung dance floor and marley. I asked for specification and got only "professional dance surface."

Rafael: That's not enough.

Nora: I'll require construction details and photos. If it isn't sprung, we need a rental floor or artistic modification.

Jules: Some sections can be adapted, but not all jumps.

Imani: Please don't leave that decision until show day.

Rafael: Deadline?

Nora: Verification by next Wednesday. If unresolved, rental quote immediately.

Rafael: Hotels. Current booking in city five has twins, but three dancers require single rooms for medical or access reasons.

Imani: I sent the confidential requirement list to you, not to the full group.

Rafael: I have it. We'll adjust rooming without circulating reasons.

Jules: Thank you.

Nora: Venue five has no laundry.

Rafael: Hotel does.

Nora: Can wardrobe use hotel machines?

Rafael: Not for performance costumes without approval. We need commercial laundry access or hand-care plan.

Jules: Some costumes are hand wash only.

Nora: I'll create a city-by-city laundry matrix.

Imani: Hydration and food. We have two venues that propose only sandwiches after show.

Rafael: Catering rider says hot meal or equivalent substantial meal.

Jules: After a performance, some dancers don't want a heavy meal immediately.

Imani: Then offer choices: hot food, carbohydrate/protein cold options, fruit, and fluids. The issue is availability, not forcing everyone to eat the same plate.

Rafael: Good.

Nora: City six involves border crossing.

Rafael: Carnets and work documentation are being prepared.

Nora: Who carries the equipment inventory?

Rafael: I do, with a duplicate on the truck.

Nora: Serial numbers for projectors and wireless equipment need to match the carnet exactly.

Rafael: We'll do a physical check before departure.

Jules: What if one costume changes between the inventory date and crossing?

Rafael: Ordinary costumes are listed by category, but high-value equipment is itemized. Any material change goes through the customs broker.

Nora: No last-minute swapping rental electronics into truck cases without telling management.

Rafael: Correct.

Imani: Rest day. There isn't one.

Rafael: I know.

Imani: Then we need protected recovery blocks. A tour can comply with contract hours and still overload people if every "free" period becomes rehearsal or press.

Jules: I can commit to no optional rehearsal on the afternoon after city three unless there is an urgent safety issue.

Rafael: Press request in city four is currently 11:00.

Imani: That's in the recovery block.

Rafael: We can send two volunteers, not the full company.

Jules: Fine.

Nora: Technical staff also need rest. Load-out crews are getting the worst schedule.

Rafael: Can we supplement with local crew at cities three and five?

Nora: Yes. If local crew handles more of the physical load-out, our touring crew can finish supervision earlier.

Rafael: Budget impact?

Nora: Less than overtime plus burnout.

Rafael: Approved.

Jules: Injury contingency. If a principal cannot perform, understudy spacing needs stage time.

Imani: But not automatically a full emergency rehearsal.

Jules: Right. We have pre-defined replacement tracks. Onstage, we need entrances, lifts, and spacing checks.

Nora: I can reserve a 20-minute contingency block before each show. If unused, it becomes extra rest.

Rafael: Excellent.

Imani: That's a better default than using rest time opportunistically.

Rafael: Communication chain?

Nora: Daily production update by 20:00 for next day:
- departure time;
- luggage call;
- venue access;
- meal schedule;
- warm-up;
- stage call;
- show;
- transport back;
- known changes.

Jules: Artistic notes should be separate. Don't bury a casting change inside the bus schedule.

Rafael: Agreed.

Imani: And medical information never goes in the general tour message.

Rafael: Yes.

Nora: One more thing: every venue asks for slightly different emergency procedures. We need local briefing.

Rafael: Five minutes at first company call: exits, assembly point, backstage access restrictions, and who is venue duty manager.

Jules: Good.

Rafael: Let's summarize principles. Freight schedule does not dictate dancer schedule. Technical work happens without performers whenever possible. Warm-up is protected. Floor safety is verified in advance. Critical gear travels redundantly. Recovery blocks are real blocks, not empty calendar space waiting to be filled.

Imani: Add that schedule changes are assessed for cumulative load, not one hour at a time.

Nora: And contingencies should be planned before the failure occurs.

Rafael: Good. I'll rebuild the master itinerary around those constraints and circulate version three tomorrow.
