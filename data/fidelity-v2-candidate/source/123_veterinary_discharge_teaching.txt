Veterinary Discharge Teaching Session

Participants:
Dr. Moreno — veterinarian
Alex — veterinary technician
Ms. Reed — dog owner

Alex: Before you head home with Milo, we're going to review the discharge plan. I'll explain the routine first, and then I want you to tell it back in your own words so we can make sure our instructions are clear.

Ms. Reed: Okay.

Dr. Moreno: Milo had surgery to repair the torn ligament in his right knee. The repair is stable, but the next several weeks depend heavily on controlled activity.

Ms. Reed: He already seems like he wants to walk normally.

Dr. Moreno: That's common. Feeling better is not the same as being healed. The bone and soft tissues need time.

Alex: For the first two weeks, activity is leash-only for bathroom trips. No running, stairs, jumping on furniture, or playing with other dogs.

Ms. Reed: My bedroom is upstairs.

Alex: Can Milo stay on the main floor temporarily?

Ms. Reed: Yes, I can sleep downstairs too.

Dr. Moreno: That's safer than repeated stairs.

Ms. Reed: What if I have to use two porch steps to get outside?

Alex: Two controlled steps with a short leash may be necessary. If he seems unsteady, use the support sling we demonstrated.

Ms. Reed: I have the sling in the bag?

Alex: Yes. Let me show you again before you leave.

Dr. Moreno: Flooring matters too. Slippery tile can cause the leg to slide.

Ms. Reed: My kitchen is tile.

Alex: Put down nonslip runners along the path he uses most.

Ms. Reed: Got it.

Alex: Medication. You have three items. I'm going to use the labels exactly.

Alex: First, the prescribed pain medication: give according to the bottle label with food unless the label says otherwise. Do not give extra doses because he seems uncomfortable.

Ms. Reed: And no human painkillers?

Dr. Moreno: Correct. Do not give ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen, aspirin, or any other human medication unless we specifically direct it. Some can be dangerous for dogs or interfere with the prescribed plan.

Alex: Second medication is the short course listed on this label. Finish it as instructed unless the clinic tells you to stop.

Ms. Reed: What if he vomits after a pill?

Alex: Call us before repeating the dose. Whether to redose depends on the medication and timing.

Dr. Moreno: That's an important rule: don't guess whether a vomited dose "counts."

Alex: Third item is the sedative for periods when activity control is difficult. Use only according to the prescribed instructions.

Ms. Reed: He gets very excited when the mail arrives. That might be when I need it.

Dr. Moreno: Possibly. Environmental management helps too: close the front blinds, use a quiet room, and limit triggers.

Alex: Incision care. The incision should stay clean and dry.

Ms. Reed: Can I put ointment on it?

Alex: No creams, peroxide, alcohol, or ointment unless we prescribe one.

Dr. Moreno: And don't bandage it yourself. We want you to look at it every day.

Alex: Normal early appearance can include mild swelling and bruising. Call us if you see increasing redness, heat, discharge, bad odor, the incision opening, or swelling that is rapidly getting larger.

Ms. Reed: He's wearing the cone full time?

Alex: Yes, unless you're directly supervising a specific activity and can absolutely prevent licking. In practice, the safest rule is cone on.

Ms. Reed: He hates it.

Dr. Moreno: Most dogs do. Licking can damage the incision very quickly, so the inconvenience is worth it.

Alex: Eating and drinking. He may have a smaller appetite tonight after anesthesia. Offer a small meal.

Ms. Reed: When do I worry?

Dr. Moreno: If he repeatedly vomits, cannot keep water down, is unusually difficult to wake, has trouble breathing, collapses, or seems severely distressed, seek urgent care. If he simply skips one small meal but is otherwise alert, call us if you're concerned, but that is less urgent.

Alex: We have the emergency hospital number printed here for times when we're closed.

Ms. Reed: What about bowel movements?

Alex: They can be delayed after fasting, anesthesia, and reduced activity. Straining, obvious pain, or prolonged problems should be discussed with us.

Dr. Moreno: Now rehabilitation timeline. At the two-week visit we assess the incision and limb use.

Ms. Reed: Do stitches come out then?

Alex: Milo's skin closure is internal, so there may be no external sutures to remove. We'll still inspect healing.

Dr. Moreno: Activity increases only after recheck guidance. Please don't add long walks because he appears energetic at day ten.

Ms. Reed: How long before normal walks?

Dr. Moreno: Recovery is staged over weeks, and the exact pace depends on follow-up exams and imaging. We'll give you each next step rather than one date today.

Alex: Physical rehabilitation referral is in your packet. They will coordinate with us.

Ms. Reed: Can I do exercises I find online?

Dr. Moreno: Please use only exercises we or the rehabilitation team recommend for Milo's stage. An exercise appropriate later may be harmful now.

Alex: Ice pack: did Dr. Moreno discuss that with you?

Ms. Reed: Yes, but I don't remember the timing.

Alex: That's why it's written here. Use the clinic's instructed duration and frequency, with a cloth barrier so cold pack isn't directly on skin. If Milo resists strongly, don't wrestle him into position.

Ms. Reed: Okay.

Alex: Let's do teach-back. Imagine you're home tonight. Milo needs to go outside. What do you do?

Ms. Reed: Short leash, just to the bathroom area. No wandering. Use the sling if he's unstable, and keep him from rushing the porch steps.

Alex: Good. He tries to jump onto the couch?

Ms. Reed: Stop him and keep him in the downstairs area where he can't access furniture.

Alex: Good. Tomorrow the incision looks a little bruised but dry.

Ms. Reed: I watch it and compare. Mild bruising can happen.

Alex: It becomes hot, much redder, and starts draining.

Ms. Reed: Call the clinic.

Dr. Moreno: Correct.

Alex: He vomits five minutes after medication and you see the pill on the floor.

Ms. Reed: I call before giving another one.

Alex: Excellent.

Dr. Moreno: What human pain medication can you give if he seems sore?

Ms. Reed: None unless you specifically tell me.

Dr. Moreno: Exactly.

Alex: How long do you keep leash restriction?

Ms. Reed: At least until the next instructions. I don't increase activity on my own just because he looks better.

Alex: Perfect.

Ms. Reed: Can I send a photo if I'm not sure about the incision?

Alex: Yes, during clinic hours. A photo can help us triage, though sometimes we may still ask you to come in.

Dr. Moreno: Keep the discharge sheet somewhere visible. Also write down medication times for the first few days; it prevents accidental double dosing when more than one person is helping.

Ms. Reed: My husband will be helping, so we'll use a chart.

Alex: Great.

Dr. Moreno: Our goal is not for you to memorize everything we said. It's for you to know the key restrictions, warning signs, and where to check or call when you're uncertain.

Ms. Reed: I feel much better after going through it this way.

Alex: Good. Before you leave, I'll fit the sling once more and make sure the cone is the right length.
